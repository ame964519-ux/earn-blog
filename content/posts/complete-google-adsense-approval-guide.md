## 8 Steps Process: How to Get Instant Google AdSense Approval for Blogger & WordPress

Follow this field-tested 8-step roadmap to guarantee your **AdSense approved** status within 48 to 72 hours:

### Step 1: Set Up Essential Legal & Trust Pages
Google’s automated compliance algorithms look for transparent site ownership and legal compliance before evaluating content. Create these 5 dedicated pages and link them in both your primary navigation and footer:
1. **About Us Page**: Include real author biographies, editorial mission statements, photographs, and professional contact details to establish strong **Google E-E-A-T** (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness).
2. **Contact Us Page**: Provide a direct business email address, a working contact form, and social media profile links.
3. **Privacy Policy Page**: Explicitly state that third-party vendors (including Google) use cookies (such as the DoubleClick DART cookie) to serve personalized ads based on past visits.
4. **Terms of Service**: Outline user conduct, intellectual property protection, and copyright disclaimers.
5. **Earnings & Affiliate Disclaimer**: Disclose any sponsored links, affiliate relationships, or promotional content in accordance with FTC guidelines.

### Step 2: Publish 18–25 Original, Search-Optimized Articles
Do not submit a website with only 4 or 5 articles. Aim for a foundational library of at least 18 to 25 articles distributed across 3 to 4 distinct categories (e.g., 5 articles per category).
- **Structure**: Utilize a clean hierarchy with H1 title, H2 main headings, and H3 subheadings.
- **Visuals**: Add custom data tables, infographics, bulleted summaries, and royalty-free images (Unsplash/Pexels) with descriptive `alt` tags.
- **Originality**: Add personal insights, real-world case studies, or step-by-step screenshots. Avoid publishing raw, unedited AI output.

### Step 3: Remove All Broken Links, Placeholders & Empty Categories
Google AdSense bots crawl every link on your domain. Ensure:
- No empty category archive pages (e.g., a category with 0 published posts triggers *"Site Behavior: Navigation"*).
- Zero 404 dead links (use a free broken link checker before applying).
- Complete removal of demo template placeholders like *"Lorem Ipsum"*, *"Hello World!"*, or sample default comments.

### Step 4: Ensure Mobile Responsiveness & Core Web Vitals Performance
Over 75% of AdSense inventory is served on mobile devices. If your mobile layout suffers from layout shifts (CLS) or slow loading times (LCP), your application may be paused.
- **Target Metrics**: Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) under **2.5 seconds**, Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) under **0.1**, and First Input Delay / INP under **200ms**.
- Implement NextGen image compression (WebP/AVIF) and utilize clean, lightweight typography.

### Step 5: Verify Google Search Console & Submit XML Sitemap
Ensure your website is registered with **Google Search Console**:
1. Verify domain ownership via DNS TXT record or HTML tag.
2. Submit your XML sitemap (e.g., `https://yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml`).
3. Verify that Google has indexed at least 12 to 20 pages organically.

### Step 6: Maintain a Clean, Uncluttered Layout
During the review window (which typically lasts 2 to 14 days), keep your layout clean. Remove aggressive pop-up overlays, push notification prompts, and lower-tier ad networks. A clean layout demonstrates that your site prioritizes reader user experience.

### Step 7: Place the Google AdSense Verification Code in `<head>`
1. Log into your Google AdSense dashboard and click **"Add Site"**.
2. Copy your unique AdSense publisher script snippet:
   ```html
   <script async src="https://pagead2.googlesyndication.com/pagead/js/adsbygoogle.js?client=ca-pub-XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX" crossorigin="anonymous"></script>
   ```
3. Paste the code between the `<head>` and `</head>` tags of your website theme (or use your platform’s dedicated header injection settings).
4. Check that your caching plugin or CDN does not block the AdSense script from executing for Google's verification crawler.

### Step 8: Submit for Review & Maintain an Active Publishing Schedule
Once you submit your application, **do not stop publishing**. Publish 2 to 3 quality articles per week while the review is pending. This signals to Google's human review team and algorithmic monitors that your website is actively maintained.

## The "Google AdSense 6 Months Rule": Myth vs Reality

A persistent topic in blogging communities is the **Google AdSense 6 months rule**.

### Where Did the Rule Come From?
Originally, Google instituted a strict policy in specific regional markets (primarily India, Pakistan, and parts of China) requiring domains to be active for at least 6 months prior to applying, aimed at curbing mass domain spam and churn-and-burn websites.

### The Reality for 2026:
Google has transitioned its approval workflow to automated machine learning models and real-time site health evaluation:
- **Custom Domains with High-Quality Content**: If your domain is registered on a custom TLD (like `.com`), has 20+ original articles, working legal pages, and verified search impressions in Google Search Console, you can get **AdSense approved in as little as 14 to 30 days**.
- **The Ideal Waiting Period**: Rather than waiting 6 months, focus on a disciplined **30-day foundation**: register your domain, publish 20 articles, verify Google Search Console indexing, and apply.

## Post-Approval Critical Step: Setting Up Your `ads.txt` File

Once your account is **AdSense approved**, you will immediately see a notification:  
> *"Earnings at risk – You need to fix some ads.txt file issues to avoid severe impact to your revenue."*

### How to Create and Deploy Your `ads.txt`:
1. Log into your Google AdSense dashboard and copy your personalized `ads.txt` snippet:
   ```text
   google.com, pub-XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX, DIRECT, f08c47fec0942fa0
   ```
2. For **Next.js / Jamstack blogs**: Place the `ads.txt` file directly inside the `/public` directory so it resolves at `https://yourdomain.com/ads.txt`.
3. For **WordPress**: Use a free file manager or the official *"Ads.txt Manager"* plugin.
4. For **Blogger (Blogspot)**: Navigate to *Settings > Monetization > Enable Custom ads.txt* and paste your line.
5. Verify in your browser by opening `https://yourdomain.com/ads.txt`. Google will automatically crawl and validate the file within 24 to 48 hours.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### How long does the Google AdSense approval process take?
The AdSense review process typically takes between **24 hours and 14 business days**. If your site requires deeper manual review, it may take up to 2 weeks. If you do not hear back after 14 days, verify that your AdSense verification code is correctly placed in your `<head>` section.

### Can I get AdSense approval on a free Blogger (`.blogspot.com`) subdomain?
Yes, Google allows native Blogger subdomains to apply via the *Earnings* tab once eligibility criteria are met. However, connecting a custom top-level domain (e.g., `yourblog.com`) achieves a **90%+ faster approval rate** and higher advertiser CPM bids.

### Can I use AI-generated content (ChatGPT/Claude) and still get approved?
Yes, Google’s official search guidance explicitly states that **the use of AI does not violate Google Search guidelines**. However, raw, repetitive AI content will trigger *"Low-Value Content"* rejections. You must edit, fact-check, add original data, and infuse human expertise into your articles before submitting.

### What should I do if my AdSense application is rejected?
1. Read the specific policy violation notice in your AdSense dashboard (*Policy Center*).
2. Fix every identified issue (e.g., add 5 new 1,500-word guides, expand thin posts, or fix broken links).
3. Ensure all updated URLs are re-indexed in Google Search Console.
4. Wait 7 to 10 days before clicking **"Request Review"** to avoid immediate automated re-rejections.

### How much monthly traffic do I need to get approved?
Google AdSense does not enforce a strict minimum traffic threshold. Sites with as few as **15 to 30 daily organic search visitors** are routinely approved, provided the content is original, valuable, and fully indexed.
